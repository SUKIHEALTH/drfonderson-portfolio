Wait and See

Created on 2021-04-09 19:15

Published on ---

At 11:15am I got a phone call on my HelloDoc phone from a distressed patient, his eye was causing him problems. He was worried about his vision.

At HelloDoc we have a daily schedule for emergencies and we can virtually see our patients almost immediately. Because we are doctors who take the call, we can assess urgency very well. Based on a few questions I decided I could see him virtually at 15:30 that same afternoon. 

An hour later, he called back, this time he had contacted a friend of his who lived in his home country. That friend said he needed to see a doctor immediately. It was not a good sign for him to have to hold his eye inside the socket. This was new for me. I asked him what he meant.

“ I feel a pressure from my eye and have to push my fingers in it so it stops”.

“Ok, I will see you in 10 minutes” I said, as this was indeed new information and worrying.

We saw each other via video call, and my first impression was that he was fine. I was fearing the worst, exophthalmos can be a serious condition when the eyeball can literally be pushed out of its socket. 

He did not have any such symptoms. When I asked him to describe what he meant by the pressure in his eyes he said “It feels itchy, then I put my fingers like this...to rub it and stop the itching…”

Well I thought, that is very different from the initial story. Pressure and Itching are two different things for a doctor, but mean the same thing for a patient. 

I sensed he was worried, and I knew that it did not require emergency attention, well not at that time. So I contacted a local optician. “Will you be so kind as to see my client, he does not have a GP here, and he is worried about his eyesight, and he wears glasses…” 

They were able to see him that same afternoon at 15:30 and at 15:50 I was contacted by the optician. “This is what I call category W, WAIT AND SEE” he said. The optician had looked at my client’s eyes, even stained it to see if there was any corneal abrasion. There was a small speck in his eyes that he could not explain, nonetheless my client was reassured and asked to return the next day for a check-up.

This is what we do at HelloDoc, assess, refer, reassure all with the use of a mobile device.
