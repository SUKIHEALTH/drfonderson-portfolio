Old habits never die

Created on 2017-06-19 17:19

Published on 2017-06-19 17:23

Every so often, I see elderly patients who are so well put together and classy, that I wish time could be turned back to their heyday.

A woman in her early nineties with a keen sense of fashion and a particularly quaint turn of phrase was admitted one evening. She wore a grey cardigan, and, underneath it, a white blouse with a delicately embroidered collar. Her pleated, brown skirt came just below her knees and matched the vest that lay folded on the back of the trolley.

Her nylon stockings had been removed by the nursing staff. ECG electrode patches were now stuck to her bare ankles. Her skin was wafer thin, making me fear what would happen should the patches be removed too quickly. She lay there waiting for the doctor to come, greeting me with a warm smile as soon as I entered. I knew she had fallen at home and dislocated her finger. Her only medication consisted of a beta‐blocker, and a statin to reduce her cholesterol level, so I felt no further tests were needed, and proceeded with her main complaint.

The little finger of her right hand had been dislocated when she caught it on the edge of an armchair during her fall. She was not in any great discomfort, she confirmed; no doubt due to the paracetamol and ibuprofen administered by a kind nurse upon arrival. ‘I still need to put it back straight, ok?’ I informed her. I counted to 3 and in a sweeping move the finger was back in its correct position. I thanked her and proceeded to walk out of the cubicle and give the ‘kind’ nurse further instructions before this patient could be discharged home.

As I pulled back the faded orange curtains of her cubicle in preparation to leave, she said, ‘Doctor, may I ask you a question?’ I nodded. ‘Yes, of course you may.’ As I approached, she gestured me nearer, as if she had a secret to tell. She pointed to her ear, urging me closer still, until I was crouching, cheek to cheek. She said, ‘How am I supposed to sponge my private parts after doing a tinkle in the lady’s privy?’ Needless to say, we arranged for a carer to assist her at home the following morning, and we did not mention how her private parts needed sponging.
