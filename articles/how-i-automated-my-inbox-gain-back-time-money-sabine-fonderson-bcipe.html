How I Automated My Inbox to Gain Back Time & Money.

Created on 2025-07-07 18:05

Published on 2025-07-08 09:00

Welcome back to my newsletter, where I write about small systems that make a big difference as a medical doctor working in a second language.

Did you know that outside social media, there is something else that quietly drains our focus every single day. That is our email inbox.

There came a moment about 2 months ago when I was staring at seriously thousands of unread emails, the vast majority of them were from subscriptions I no longer found interesting.

I did unsubscribe, I even created filters to catch certain keywords and send them straight to the bin, but despite all those efforts they kept coming. More and more.

I had no more digital space let alone mental space to process them all.

It was when I was hit with a warning that my memory was running out and needed to pay or declutter in order not to lose access to my emails. It just meant that I would no longer receive emails.

That would not be such a bad idea, but it did not solve my problem.

The problem

There were over 5,000 unread emails in my inbox and that just irritated me.

Does that sound familiar?

After reading the book on changing my habits I decided to spend some time deleting emails. But then 30 to 40 minutes would pass and the needle didn't shift.

The real problem wasn't volume, it was fragmentation, nothing was in one place so I could cleary decide what to do with important emails.

When I looked at emails, I realized that many that came through my inbox I had once upon a time long ago subscribed to something or other. Little did I know that I also gave permission to other companies to flood my inbox with some sales pitch.

I noticed that my mail would categorize already for me and divide emails into forums, social, updates, promotions and inbox.

It took a while but some updates had important emails especially with attachments and these included updates on travel iteniary or updates on a purchase I made.

But the worst thing, is that in the clutter of emails, I just did not see vital messages. Until I would get a third reminder of an unpaid invoice. Ouch how embaracing.

It's not that I couldn't pay, I just didn't see the email. I wonder if anyone would really believe that?

Something had to chance.

The solution

So I built a solution that worked for me.

And it was for free. For the first time in many months I did not have to subscribe to a monthly instalment to have my emails organized. I could actually write a script that runs in Google Workspace and connects Gmail to Google Sheets and Google Drive. What it does is really simple actually, and I can't believe it took me this long to figure it out

  • It finds emails with attachements in my inbox and updates

  • Filters out junk or duplicates

  • Saves the attachment to a folder in my Google Drive so I can never say "It got lost in the clutter"

  • Logs everything - the sender, subject, date, tags into a Google spreadsheet

  • And then - this is the fun part - my script uses AI to summarize the email and suggest next steps.

So instead of worrying about the clutter, I can worry about what is really important and needs my attention.

The script runs automatically, on a schedule, no more clicking, no more scrolling endlessly for emails and no more dragging files around. 

What if feels like to see something work
That feeling is amazing. When I open my google sheet I see a nice overview table. Filename, sender, Summary, Status. 
I know what needs my attention and I can set the status to "Done"  or " Trashed". And if something needs a reply or appointment, the script can detect that too.
The mental relief is real. And I no longer need to wonder "Did I save that file?".
It's logged, out of sight, and no longer cluttering my inbox.
Who can use it?
This tool isn't just for doctors. Just because I happen to be one who gets over 100 emails per day in 2 different languages, I made it work for me.
If you are a VA who is managing the inbox of your (healthcare) client juggling multiple. Or you are a clinicial working across multiple roles - this script gives you one place to land and process emails.
It also respects your time, because you are in control 
I allow no more than 50 emails per week that I process on the google Sheet. As long as it gets done and I can see my inbox number go down and down, I feel a rest come over me.
Plus it reduces the risks of missing something important becasue it got buried in a long thread. 
For me building tools like this is part of staying connected to the work I care about - without burning out from all the noise around it.
If this sounds like something your workflow needs, check out the script and setup guide in the comments.
