Sorry to deceive you, but I am not blonde.

Created on 2015-12-21 18:47

Published on 2015-12-21 19:25

Securing a training post is the highlight of any junior doctor's career. It is an affirmation of their role as a future specialist. 

The route to that training post is not an easy one. A colleague was kind enough to share with me his journey.

He went to his interview full of self assurance. According to him, he 'aced' the clinical skills station. Then came the general knowledge and communication skills. Sitting opposite 3 interviewers he recalled how one interviewer in particular, sat tapping her foot impatiently on the floor and was looking at her watch. But he still managed to answer all the questions and felt confident at the end. 

The next day, looking forward to a positive result, he got the shock of his life. His application had been rejected.

A consultant who was just as sure that he was a highly suitable candidate and was to get the post was equally shocked. It did not take him long to get the name of one of the interviewers and he made a phone call to ask 'Why?'

He received a long list of excuses:  'It was not their fault...he was actually a fine candidate...they were hurried because other interviews had gone on too long...'

The excuse that stuck out the most according to my colleague was when they said 'he had not sold himself'. 

Needless to say, a year later, he not only sold himself like a marketing guru, he out performed and was crowned the best applicant they had ever come across. 

When he told me his story, it was one of  bitter sweet victory. Although he was happy to finally be in a training post, he could put the 'whole ordeal' behind him...the system failed him and not the other way round. 

So I kept my experience to myself and praised him for finally securing that well deserved post. And I wondered how he would have felt if he had gone through what I went through after an interview a few years ago.  

After my interview, I was promptly told that I had not been selected so I asked 'Why?'

The response I got will stay with me forever. 

'When we looked at your CV we were expecting a blond Swedish candidate. The name on this application does not sound ethnic.'
