The Skill of Coaching Emotional Workflow in a New Medical System

Created on 2025-05-07 05:52

Published on 2025-05-08 13:28

She calls the practice almost every week, sometimes twice. Not because of a medical emergency, but because her anxiety tells her there might be one.

This time, her concern was an upcoming knee operation. She was afraid she hadn’t told the anesthesiologist everything. Specifically, that she sometimes loses control of her bladder — something she’s deeply ashamed of.

“It happens when I’m anxious and alone,” she said. “But never when I’m out with my sister.”

That moment held something powerful. Her body was communicating something her words couldn’t quite express.

As we talked through her fears, I noticed something else. She interrupted herself over and over with the same phrase:

“Sorry.”

Sorry for talking too much. Sorry for being emotional. Sorry for needing help.

Like many patients with chronic anxiety, her default language was apology. She’d been told countless times to stop saying sorry and yet, no one had given her anything to replace it with.

So I offered her a different framework:

“You’re allowed to say sorry. But let’s make it intentional. Create a ‘sorry jar.’ Every time you say the word, drop in a marble. And if you can, try saying: ‘Thank you for listening.’ ‘I appreciate your time.’ ‘I feel better sharing this with you.’”

Because words matter. “Sorry” reinforces guilt. “Thank you” fosters connection, dignity, and calm.

For the first time in over a year, I saw a twinkle in her eye. Not because we solved her medical problem, but because we gave her a tool she could own.

The Lesson for Doctors Navigating a New Medical System

I have worked for many years as a doctor practicing medicine in a second and sometimes third language. And just like my patient, I have been overwhelmedd by the emotional and structural expectations of a new healthcare system. At the same time I had to

  • Reassure patients in a language that is not my own

  • Process alot of information under pressure

  • Avoid full blown anxiety attacks while building trust with the patients who needed me the most

This patient’s story is a reminder: Workflow isn’t just about efficiency. It’s also about language, emotional structure, and cultural safety.

When we learn to embed that into our consultations even in a new language, we work smarter, connect better, and protect our own well-being along the way.
