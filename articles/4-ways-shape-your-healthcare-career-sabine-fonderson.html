4 ways to shape your healthcare career

Created on 2018-10-17 20:55

Published on 2018-10-17 21:09

Picture your career as that represented by the Dutch artist M.C Escher’s Relativity graphic art. A career in healthcare feels like a dimension of various staircases, at times you move up, down, diagonally or not at all. It can cause confusion as you navigate your way towards various roles.

You can be looking for your ideal post and yet your previous experience and skills may not at first glance align to that position. So what 4 questions should you answer now in order to start shaping your career to match your ideal job?

  1. Do I have the right skills for the job?
  2. If I do not have all the right skills, what am I willing to do in order to obtain them?
  3. Do I have the ability and approach to do the work to keep me motivated even when it drains energy from me?
  4. Do I really understand my employer’s needs?

In answering the first question, list skills and competencies that you have that come naturally for you. It might not be a criteria of skills required for your ideal job, but your natural strengths are vital to show you have transferable skills.

For the second question, you need to stay true to your values. A major part of shaping your career is professional development. You need to actively keep up to date with industry changes, research and developing (supplementary) skills. You also need to be willing to do what it takes to develop these skills.

The third and fourth answers should highlight options such as

  • Where you want to be in a few years and the realistic steps you need to get there
  • Whether you are willing to sacrifice for instance pay over professional status
  • How much will it take to establish your role within the organisation

When I went for an interview at a previous employer, it became clear that I had transferable skills and a high eagerness to learn new skills in the role. However, on paper my skills did not completely match the job requirements, particularly my daily tasks. Despite my enthusiasm and eagerness to show how my diverse experience and skills could be put to use, I encountered various setbacks. A few months into the role I became less motivated. I also realised that staying true to my values meant realising that actually this was not a match nor was this my ideal job. Once that realisation set in, I found that I was no longer committed to adapting my approach in keeping my work interesting and stimulating.

So how did this experience help shape my career? I changed my approach. M.C. Escher’s artwork ‘relativity’ took on another perspective. Even-though I felt confused and did not have a set path, the direction my career was taking was the right one at that time. It was necessary to shape me into a new role.

A career in healthcare is set to change. Knowing how to deal with such changes is important. Use the 4 questions above to help shape your career at that moment.
