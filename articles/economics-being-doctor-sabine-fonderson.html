The economics of being a doctor

Created on 2016-02-24 22:08

Published on 2016-02-24 23:04

It is the end of a month, there are piles of envelopes I do not want to open because I know that one in 3 of them will remind me of a payment that is overdue.

Things can get worse I tell myself, but unfortunately with the new contracts and salary cuts it is getting worse. Due to personal circumstances I am not able to complete a training post and hence I freelance as an emergency medicine doctor in the UK. The benefits have been well appreciated such as I have more flexibility along with the ability to schedule my work around my life and not the other way round. However with the drastic cuts on locum pay rates which came into effect just a few months ago, my direct debits payments have not appreciated the direct negative consequences at the end of each month since then.

In real terms my pay has gone down by 35% from one day to the next. No meetings to talk about it, no warnings in advance to see how these changes could affect my life. Nope, the salary cuts have simply been imposed and enforced. And in just a few months, locum pay cuts are set to go down by a further 15%.

So where does that leave me financially?

In the red.... The fact remains that regardless of the imposed contract changes in the UK, a drop in salary was not part of the grand scheme of things when I pursued a career as a doctor. Benevolent as it is to help other people, it comes at a price of spending countless of hours, weeks and months studying in order to get the skills to do just that. Part of the time I spent in medical school and during rotation was financed with the aid of government based student loans. A loan that I am more than happy to pay back. Student loans aside, what about the incremental costs of living, childcare, malpractice insurance, courses and well just the basic costs of staying alive?

Maybe if I did major in Economics at university I would have been able to predict such outcomes. Common sense tells me that I am now forced to reinvent my lifestyle. Starting over again seems daunting but I know I have to face 3 hard truths

1. If I thought my salary is affected now wait what a career change will do to my take home income next. A complete nose dive for the first few years surely.

2. Changing careers may mean sacrificing my current living location. The costs of relocating may not be covered by the new employer.

3. The grass looks greener on the other side. An option would be to to hang in there and suffer in economic silence.

What are my options at the end of the day?

I am not entirely sure, but it is something I will be exploring in the coming months. Why shouldn't I? Nothing to lose I tell myself except another late payment and maybe even my car.
