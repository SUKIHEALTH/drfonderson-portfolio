Built in 45 minutes: Now my chaos has a calendar. Why I built this voice todo app.

Created on 2026-06-22 11:54

Published on 2026-06-22 12:21

It is not a secret by now. I work as a doctor and own a general practice, all that navigating in a second language. After 30 consultations, meetings, deadlines and appointments, the phone calls and lab results still need a place in my agenda and my clinic day is far from over.

I made a choice just over a year ago to not bring my work with me. But that doesn't mean that it still needs to be chaotic in my head.

Because there are moments on the car ride home when i remember something "don't forget to call the moving company" or "get that document over to the financial advisor".

I got pretty tired of people sending me reminders. It is not their responsibility to keep me on track.

The work is not the problem, the mental load of trying to remember everything is.

I did not want another to-do app with 47 buttons and a login.

I wanted to just talk, even maybe rant for 60 seconds max, like a slightly unhinged GP and have something turn that mess into actual tasks - with dates - and drop them straight into my calendar.

So I built it. I have some coding background, I used my Chromebook and a coffee.

I press one button, talk for max 1 minute (which for me is too long). Whisper transcribes it. The AI tool listens to my chaos, pulls out the real tasks, guesses sensible dates, and sorts them by category. And out comes a calendar file. With another tap, it is in my google calendar with reminders.

The brain feels empty already. All this in the time to make a banana bread for the team lunch scheduled the next day.

The one thing you can use today

You don't need to build an app to steal this idea. Tonight, instead of lying there rehearsing what you need to say when you forget an appointment or a deadline, open your phone's voice recorder and rant for 60 seconds, in which ever language you want. Then paste the transcript into your own AI tool with this prompt:

That is it, the entire prompt. Your phone already has enough tools in it to make your life more organised. 
Remember. This is for your own agenda, not patient data. Do not dictate identifiable patient information into a personal recorder or any consumer AI tool. That is a different conversation entirely, within different medico-legal rules. As a practitioner, I have obligations and I don't bend them just because the tool is convenient. 
It will also not fix an overloaded system. It just stops the overload from following me to my pillow.
The most expensive thing in my week is  not missing a task, is the energy and pressure I feel after I miss it. So I built this tool, to use it daily and my agenda is filling up with little to do-lists and I love it because i have some accountability.
