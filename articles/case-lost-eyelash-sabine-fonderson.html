The case of a lost eyelash

Created on 2017-11-30 11:03

Published on 2017-11-30 11:15

On the rare occasion that I pick up a phone call at the emergency department, I do so on the red phone with the special ring‐tone. When this phone goes off, doctors and nurses in the immediate vicinity stop what they’re doing and try to listen in on what is being said. The dialogue is usually abrupt, and ends with the caller stating the ETA (estimated time of arrival) to advise how long doctors, nurses and other staff have to get to the resuscitation room before the urgent and potentially life‐threatening case arrives.

This night shift had been unusually calm. I remember that because I was catching up on a few discharge letters to patients’ family doctors, when, you’ve guessed it, the red phone rang. I grabbed the receiver and, to my amazement, heard the barely discernable babble of a frantic woman. It took some time to calm her down enough to make out what she was saying.

‘I went to an eye doctor earlier today to get some eye drops. Tonight my husband put some of the drops in my eye and noticed I have a hair in my eye.’

‘You have a hair in your eye?’ I asked.

‘Yes, I closed my eyes and now I’m worried that the hair has travelled up into my eye and, oh dear, what am I supposed to do?’

‘Madam, you’re through to the emergency service line. This is for emergencies only. How did you get this number?’ I asked. At this point a few nurses had gathered by the phone to listen in on the conversation. Was this a joke, or were we listening to a real situation on the emergency line?

‘I was connected to you. What are you going to do about my eyelash? My husband is certain it’s stuck in my eye. But I just don’t know how to get it out,’ she yelped, as if holding back tears.

‘Is your eye still closed?’

‘Uh huh,’ she muttered. Just then her husband whispered something, and she added, ‘Do you think we need to call an ambulance?’

I tried to muster up the courage to tell this patient, as politely as I could at 3 am in the morning, that I could not make that decision for her over the phone. I implored her to use her sound judgement. Perhaps with the help of her devoted husband, the two of them could try to remove the eyelash. If she felt the need to use the emergency department then it would be her decision. I reminded her that she should not have been connected to this line as it needed to be clear for emergency cases.

There was great anticipation. Wagers were placed. Who would win the bet that this woman would be rushed to us in an ambulance trolley, requesting gas and air for a dislodged eyelash? The money raised went to a good cause, and we all enjoyed pizzas on the next night shift – hurray!
