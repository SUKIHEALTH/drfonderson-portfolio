How I build a Situgram-helper app and saved 60 minutes on a learning assessment.

Created on 2025-10-19 18:49

Published on 2025-10-20 09:15

Last week, I stared at a 7-page PDF learning assessment and thought:

"There has to be a better way."

As a GP and epidemiologist working in the Netherlands, I'm used to juggling patient care, research, and continuous professional development. Like many of you reading this, I often work in my second language, which adds an extra layer of cognitive load to everything I do.

The assignment? Complete a "Situgram" assessment. This is a 15-question tool designed to identify your learning preferences.

In an effort to submit my assignment two days before the due date, I had to read a concept I've never heard of before. Part of understanding this meant manually fillin in scores on a pdf file, calculating the totals across five categories then drawing the results on bar chart and if I made a calculation mistake doing it all over again.

For busy doctors already stretched thin, this 60-minute exercise felt like an unnecessary barrier to valuable self-insight.

So I decided to build a solution.

The Problem: Cognitive Overload in Learning

Here's what struck me: the tool itself was creating the exact opposite of what it was meant to assess.

The Situgram measures five learning preferences:

- Ka (Kunst afkijken) - Learning by observing what works

- Pa (Participeren) - Learning through dialogue

- Kv (Kennis verwerven) - Learning through acquiring knowledge

- Oe (Oefenen) - Learning through safe practice

- Od (Ontdekken) - Learning by diving into the deep end

My learning preference? Ontdekken (discover) and Kunst afkijken (observe what works). I learn best by jumping into new challenges and seeing what successful people do.

Yet here I was, being asked to slowly, methodically fill out forms, the exact opposite of how I learn best.

The irony was not lost on me.

The Solution: A weekend project becomes a 5-minute app

I spent a weekend building an app that turns this 60-minute paper exercise into a 5-minute interactive experience.

Here’s how it cuts through the complexity and respects a busy doctor's time:

  1. Progressive Disclosure: One question at a time. For non-native speakers, cognitive chunking is everything. Less fatigue, better focus.

  2. Visual Feedback: Color-coded answers mean you see patterns (like your dominant preference) immediately, without re-reading instructions.

  3. Instant, Accurate Calculation: The app automatically sums scores, applies the standardization formula, and generates an interactive bar chart. Automation equals speed and accuracy.

  4. One-Click Export: Download your results instantly for your portfolio or supervisor.

I was motivated by a real problem, had a free weekend, and the help of an amazing AI-programming tool (no, not ChatGPT). My Ontdekken preference kicked in, and I learned by building.

If you’re a professional in the Netherlands who uses the Situgram, or are simply tired of archaic paper-based assessments, this is for you. I hope it helps you focus on the insight, not the process.
