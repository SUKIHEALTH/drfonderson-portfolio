How I Generated Anatomy Art for deeper Patient Conversations

Created on 2025-07-13 14:18

Published on 2025-07-14 07:00

People underestimate how much the physical space of a consultation room matters. The walls are generally silent. Some clinic walls depict personal stories of the physician from family portraits to diploma's.

I’m not an artist, but I’ve always appreciated vibrant colours and I’ve long felt that clinical settings leave too much blank space. Mine does.

One day, a patient stared at one of the two diplomas on my wall, then said I looked younger than my age. Another looked confused, squinting to fully understand what an ultrasound abdominal diploma meant.

It started as a technical assignment. As part of my learning in AI and LLMs, I was asked to generate an image. That is when it hit me, I would generate images to put on my boring clinic wall. Something warmer, something that could trigger other conversations.

When I got to coding, I did what typically happens when AI takes over. I went down a rabbit hole because what I generated sparked a chain reaction in my "creative" mind.

The first anatomy image I created was really awesome. Some will say that "this isnt' art". I get it, art is a journey, a story created by an artist. I am not an artist. But it was artful enough for me to appreciate the colours and more importantly the emotion it evoked in me. I looked at it and thought: this should be on my wall.

So I kept going.

I built a small engine that takes any anatomical part and generates a bold, vibrant poster in various styles. Then it creates a mockup automatically, complete with a watermark. Each piece is saved and logged.

No two are the same. So it is art?

Initially I struggled to put the art on my wall. What if someone asked, where did you get this, or who created this?

It doesn't matter if it was created by an AI tool, what matters is that it is a visual tool to create a feeling once I or patients walk into my clinic. Warmth, curiosity and a sense of safety.

The feedback so far, mostly from colleagues and family has been simple: “Wow, that’s cool.” That’s enough for me to keep going.

I created a webapp that allows you to create any anatomy artwork in the given styles.

Here is how it works and you can also get a look at some of the mockups I created earlier.

Want to explore the tool? Here it is. You will need an #openai API key for it to work.

The link takes you to Hugging Face Spaces, the platform that hosts this tool. It's a developer-friendly way to deploy Gradio apps - but it does come with some constraints

  • You need a requirements.txt or it won't run

  • You need an app.py file even though I cretated the entire code from a Jupyter Notebook

  • All mockup files had to be included and refrenced locally - that took a while to figure out

  • The OPENAI must be correct otherwise it will crash silently. That is why I built a personal API key input so that users can bring their own key and keep generating safely. Keys are not copied nor stored, it is safe.

The app is live, you can enter

  • An anatomy term like lung

  • A style you like - if you can't find the style in the list just add it after anatomy

  • A watermark, even though you didn't generate it, you can add your mark because you visualised it

  • Your OpenAI key

In a matter of seconds you will get a framed poster mockup of an art that you can hang on a wall or even use as a greeting cards for your patients.

Tools like these make my life as a doctor working in a second language fun and allows me to appreciate the daily challenges in a clinic setting.
