How I'm building an on-boarding tool to save time and retain talent in primary care

Dr. Sabine Fonderson

Physician · Health AI Innovator · Keynote Speaker

The real doctor
Helping doctors rebuild their careers after immigration.

Created on 2026-05-12 20:25

Published on 2026-05-13 06:30

Let me tell you something that doesn't get talked about enough. The nightmare called on-boarding talent in general practice.

I'm a GP. I practice medicine every day in my second language. And if you've never had to explain a complex diagnosis, navigate a difficult conversation with a patient, or work through an emergency, all while your brain is simultaneously translating, you cannot imagine how exhausting that is.

But I didn't build this tool because of my own story. I built it because I kept seeing the same thing happen around me.

The talent problem nobody is solving

According to the LHV's latest labour market survey, nearly half of all GP practices in the Netherlands are actively searching for new staff and 16% of current GPs expect to retire within five years.

But here's what I know as a practice owner: hiring more people isn't the solution if we keep losing them to burnout, overwhelm, and a broken onboarding experience. The real crisis isn't recruitment. It's retention.

And nowhere is this more painful than with doktersassistenten (medical assistants).

When a practice loses a medical assistant, someone who knows the systems, knows the patients, knows how things work, it doesn't just create a gap on the roster. It creates chaos. The remaining team absorbs the pressure. Quality dips. And the new person who comes in? They're handed a folder, told to shadow someone who's already stretched too thin, and expected to figure it out.

That's not onboarding. That's survival mode.

Here's what I also see though.

There are so many talented people who want to work in primary care.

Medical professionals who are motivated, smart, and ready to grow but who need a structured way to get clinical experience. People who are maybe switching roles, re-entering the workforce, or like me, navigating a system that wasn't originally designed for them.

The bottleneck isn't talent. The bottleneck is time and capacity to train that talent.

Most GP practice I know wants to onboard someone. The LHV data backs this up, practices recognise the importance of training new staff, but they simply don't have the capacity or the space to do it properly. So they don't. And the cycle continues.

So I started building something

The idea is simple: what if a new employee could ask any question about your practice's protocols, about medical guidelines, about how you handle a specific situation and get an instant, accurate answer at any time of day?

Not a Google search. Not asking a colleague mid-consultation. Not a folder nobody can find.

An assistant for the assistant that actually knows your practice. That speaks your language. That is available at 7am when someone is prepping for their first shift and has no one to ask.

Here's how it works:

  • A practice uploads their own protocols and procedures

  • The AI learns from them instantly.

  • Built-in from day one: all medical protocols and guidelines and standard intake flows

  • New staff ask questions, get answers, build confidence

The result? Faster onboarding. Less pressure on experienced staff. And a real chance for motivated people to learn and grow, without needing someone to hold their hand every step of the way.

Let me be transparent:

This is not finished. Not even close.

There are real challenges ahead, privacy compliance (practice ownership), protocols licensing, building trust with healthcare professionals who are rightly skeptical of AI. I'm not pretending those are small things.

But I've gone from idea to live demo in a few days. And as I grow my practice and team, I will be using it to see how it works in real-life.

What's next?

  • Connect real document intelligence so the AI actually learns from uploaded files

  • Load in real Dutch Primary Care (NHG) guidelines and test with actual clinical questions

  • Find 1-2 pilot practices willing to test this with their team

  • Talk to people navigating privacy compliance in healthcare AI

  • Explore innovation funding through bigger organizations

If any of this resonates with you, I want to hear from you.

Whether you work in primary care, you've built something in healthcare tech, you've dealt with compliance issues, or you're one of those talented professionals looking for a way into the clinical world, let's talk.

This is early. It's messy. But the problem is real, the timing is right, and I genuinely believe we can build something that changes how primary care grows its people.
