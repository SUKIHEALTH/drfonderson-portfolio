Financial planning for rainy days. The role of Umbrella services for freelance doctors

Created on 2016-01-19 05:22

Published on 2016-01-20 10:42

Freelance doctors in the UK  can earn lots of money...on paper. What they fail to realize is that without effective planning they risk major financial set backs. 

This article highlights the role of an umbrella service and how certain financial blunders freelance doctors make can be avoided. 

In a nutshell, umbrella services act as your employer and under their infrastructure you should benefit from insurance and employment rights. They also make sure that your payments are processed in a timely manner according to the timesheets you submit. Most important, umbrella services make sure you pay the right amount of taxes and contribute to your National Insurance (NI).

Umbrella services send your timesheet over to the locum agency. Once accorded the umbrella services gets paid and pay you directly into your personal account on a PAYE scheme. This method is ideal for doctors who are considering short-term freelance work to bridge the gap between their substantive post and other commitments.

Umbrella services are beneficial in that they take much of the financial and administrative burden off your hands. Another benefit is that once you have made an expense, you will be happy to know that you will be fully reimbursed. Sometimes within a week or two after submitting the receipts. 

Don't think that everything you spend your hard earned money on will be reimbursed. There are guidelines to follow.

For example, you cannot declare your mortgage or rent as an expense. As a freelance doctor it is assumed that you will be doing your work outside your home. However, you can declare accommodation costs if you spend a few nights away from home. Added to that, you can also declare travel expenses and certain meals.

Other costs such as a new stethoscope, medical books and even courses can be reimbursed.  A note about courses. You will be reimbursed on a course you pay for if that course enhances an already existing skill you have. So let us say you are a freelance A&E doctor and want to do a course on x-ray diagnosis and ECG readings. These courses can be reimbursed because they will help you perform better during your work.

However, let's say you are tired of A&E and want to transition to another career and you decide to take on a course to help with that transition. According to the HMRC,  this will not be reimbursed. In effect you will be acquiring new knowledge and skills that have nothing to do with your current freelance work. So be careful when you book courses left and right, as tempting as they are, they do not all meet the criteria of reimbursement.

Umbrella services take away the burden of doing your own finance and that can be a blessing for many freelance doctors. Keep in mind that their services are based on a yearly contract. You pay a certain fee per month for their services. 

One of the disadvantages of the this contract is when you want to stop freelancing.  Read the fine prints, because if you break the contract before the term is done you will most likely be asked to pay the remainder of the contract in one go.

If doctors want a hands-off approach to their financial administration then an umbrella service may be the best fit for them at the moment. In either case, here is a check-list to serve you when choosing an umbrella service:

  1. Is the umbrella service a member of a professional body? Many umbrella services have been shut down by HMRC because they fail to comply to legislations. If an umbrella service guarantees you will take home something like 90%, then there is a big chance that they are working off-shore and are using a tax-avoidance scheme. Should HMRC shut such an umbrella service down, you will also be put under investigation and may have to repay taxes.
  2. Do the umbrella service cover for insurance? Indemnity insurance is standard that they should offer. Read the fine prints to make sure that this is covered in the fee you pay.
  3. Does the umbrella service charge a fixed rate or a variable rate? Typically they will charge between 2-5% of your salary per month. Anything lower would most likely mean you have very poor customer services and anything higher, then you might as well set up your private limited company and do it yourself or get an accountant.
  4. Does the umbrella service try to lure you by giving you grandiose hopes of claiming all types of expenses? 

Whether an umbrella service or your own accountant, there are important reasons why a freelance doctor must choose to have a licensed entity that manages their financial accounts particularly if this is something you do not see yourself spending your precious weekends doing.

Remember, the costs of getting your taxes wrong are high and there is no running from the tax collector.
