How I Created a Podcast Series to Empower Virtual Assistants (VAs) in Healthcare in under 2 hours.

In today’s healthcare landscape, innovation isn’t just optional, it’s how we survive. Between patient demands, documentation, performance metrics, and the quiet pressure of always proving your competence especially in a second language it can feel like the current system can't help but exhaust the people working in it.

That’s why I’ve never waited for innovation to trickle down. I’ve built it into how I work.

Recently, I wrote about a Chrome extension I developed to automate safety-netting notes. Soon after, I created another tool this time to prevent staff from miscalculating average blood pressure readings. Both small tools. But each one solved a real problem, fast.

Still, the bigger question kept coming up: What happens when AI starts doing the work you used to delegate?

Back to the Beginning: My First VA

My relationship with Virtual Assistants began in 2008. Fresh out of med school, I launched a business called ASHIA A Student’s Help in Africa. Medical students were eager to volunteer, and I wanted to offer something structured, something that changed my life - internships in Africa. In order to do so I built a website, a workflow using Gmail, Google Docs, and eventually hosted a podcast with guest speakers from all over the world. I even set up a Facebook page and a basic CRM.

Then I listened to a podcast by Pat Flynn on hiring VAs from the Philippines. That changed everything. Within weeks, I had a VA working 6 hours a week, managing my inbox, handling social media, and filtering student inquiries. She was professional, consistent, and gave me back the one thing every founder and doctor lacks: space to think.

We worked together for three years. We even started other businesses together. Some worked. Some didn’t. Life moved on.

Fast Forward: My Practice Today

A year ago, I realized I needed that support again. Running a general practice with just over 3000 patients, leading a growing team and working in a system that demands more than it supports I once again needed a buffer.

My VA helped me transition into the role, managed repetitive admin, coordinated patient queries. Until one day, the tasks started disappearing.

Not because they weren’t needed but because AI could handle them faster and in some cases, better. She noticed it too. “Maybe we should create a chatbot for the clinic,” she said casually during one of our weekly check-ins. That’s when it hit me.

If AI is replacing the busywork, what happens to the person who used to do it?

Reimagining the Role

Rather than letting her become obsolete, I decided to upskill her not in healthcare, but in understanding how to use AI to support healthcare. That’s where the content engine came in.

I started to gather information from trusted sources and my own notes on AI. Instead of writing stiff, formal training materials, I used Gemini 1.5 Flash to turn it into podcast-style conversations between two fictional hosts. Natural, engaging, zero jargon.

Once the scripts were ready, I used ElevenLabs to generate the audio. Their voice cloning tool allowed me to speak once, and it did its magic. The output was good. Not perfect. Slightly robotic. But more than usable. Paired with a Notion workbook, it became a real system. A way to learn, take action, and keep growing together. In less than 3 hours, we were able to upload 6 podcasts.

Why This Matters

This isn’t about creating perfect content. This is about staying functional as the ground shifts beneath us.

AI is moving fast. VAs are evolving. And doctors like myself, working across cultures, languages, systems don’t have time to start from scratch every time something changes.

This workflow allowed me to:

  • Teach my VA how to support AI in a medical setting

  • Turn abstract tools into practical, patient-facing strategies

  • Free up even more time without compromising on care

Because the truth is: every hour saved on admin is an hour I can spend thinking clearly, communicating better, or simply... resting.

What’s Next

This was just the beginning. We are now building a full toolkit for doctors and their VAs to adapt to this next phase of clinical work. Not just a podcast. Not just a workbook. A full system to help doctors in multilingual, high-pressure environments use AI without feeling lost in the hype.

If you’re working with a VA and want them to work with AI or thinking about hiring one this could be the right time to redefine what that role can look like.

Because once the inbox is automated, the real work begins. You will need a VA who remains critical and is that human filter that makes interaction important.
