My first telemedicine experience

Created on 2017-12-20 09:01

Published on 2017-12-20 09:58

In 2015 I was approached by two young entrepreneurs to be part of their journey in e-health. The platform these two brothers have founded is called Doctor Pocket (DP) and I am so happy that they saw in me what I now see in myself...the potential to grow and be part of the (inevitable) evolution of telemedicine.

The demand for consulting a doctor online regarding child health needs further research. Nonetheless, it is clear that because children today use and understand mobile devices, we need to make sure that telemedicine is adapted to their demands and that of the parents who will most likely initiate the consultations.

The technology needs to be developed in such a way that as children grow older, they are not only aware of the possibility that e-health has to offer but that they should be encouraged from a certain age to actively take a role in managing their health. With this comes a great sense of responsibility to the child, however communicating one's health issue is something that needs to be taught from early in a child's life.

So telemedicine should go beyond just creating a virtual arena for interaction between doctors and patients. In particular for children, there needs to be allowance for interaction that takes into account their beliefs, values and how they think and feel concerning their health. If parents can teach their children to select videos on a tablet, then they can be taught to communicate health-related issues to key actors (teachers, counsellors, doctors, care-givers etc).

My first telemedicine experience came when a mother asked me for tips on what to give her 8 week old infant who had difficulties feeding, there was no weight loss, but her infant had spent a few days without moving her bowels.

In this age group the most common questions posed by parents involve things like feeding, weaning, vaccinations, sleeping patterns, crying patterns and motor development.

The e-consult with the mother in question lasted altogether 35 minutes and for me the most important thing about this experience was leaving the mother with certain options or scenarios on what to do should my advice not be effective for her little girl. I think I did this because I relied heavily on the information that mother was giving me. For me to get that information, I asked many questions. Without the physical examination, the e-consultation between doctor and patient may take on a different dimension in doctor-patient relationship. However, this is a topic for another time.
