How changes in healthcare can affect your employability: 3 lessons learned

Dr. Sabine Fonderson

Physician · Health AI Innovator · Keynote Speaker

Created on 2018-10-10 14:35

Published on 2018-10-10 14:48

Employability is more than just getting a job. Employability is also more than just having a particular set of skills. Employability is about being aware of the changes in the healthcare landscape and being able to work within this changing environment, despite the demands and challenges.

Recently I spoke to a medical doctor who trained as a GP and currently works in a group practice. In the past years he felt that he could offer more value to his patients than by being just ‘another GP’ within the practice. The piling paperwork and bureaucratic challenges drained his energy and the passion he once had for his work. He noticed that his patients needed more time during consultations and he enjoyed giving them that time. But felt the pressure. He had to see each of his patients within a set time frame, less than 10 minutes. This led to him frequently worrying about the ‘hasty’ decisions he made. At times he laid awake at night thinking if his patients were ok.  The time came when he needed to revalidate his GP licence. It was a tough decision for him. He needed to learn that part of making a decision was about knowing who he was and how it would align with his future at the practice.

Finally he told me,  he decided not to revalidate. This infuriated many of his colleagues. He was not surprised at some of the comments he received

‘what are you doing?’

‘why are you wasting your time...?’

‘do you know how hard it is to become a GP all over again…?’

The pressure was again high. He could have easily given in to it, but he remained true to himself this time. He said

‘I want to be able to provide the care I know I can give to my patients. They need more time, I need more time. I really enjoy having that time with them, to explain to them how hormones work at the molecular level, for instance. To show them in detail why taking this medication is better than the other. They thank me for the time and I see a lot of improvement.’

Believing his time at the group practice may be limited due to his pending revalidation, he started to search for job positions that would give him that opportunity to have more time with his patients. Unfortunately none were available. A post for  ‘doctor needed who can spend 1 hour  with patients at practice’ simply did not exist.

However, when an opportunity arose for him to develop his knowledge and skills within the area of orthomolecular & integrative health, he took it. He attended lectures and seminars. He got involved in network groups and even completed a course and gained several certificates in the field. Within a year, he had formulated his own position within the group practice and grew his patient load from 2 per week to over 15 per day.

His passion, drive and proactiveness led him to focus on a group of patients that needed more time. Something he and even his colleagues thought was not possible in the current medical landscape.  It proved to be a success and he has found the energy needed to do his work with joy.

Within the changing world of healthcare, resilience and a strong belief in your abilities are essential to continue working as a professional. That one job title you are searching for everyday will not exist. In fact, employers are often looking for someone outside their organisation while the perfect candidate is staring them in the face. So dare to look beyond the basic needs of the job requirements, enhance your employability by following these 3 steps:

  1. Continue to develop professionally and personally despite a challenging and difficult work environment. This shows you are flexible and highly adaptable  
  2. Become familiar with external factors that may affect your employability. For instance know that economic, political, environmental and social factors are real issues in healthcare and can change priorities within your organisation
  3. Be comfortable knowing that as you make that transition, your career identity will change as well over a period of time. Don’t just limit your time to skills and work experience, build a social and professional network as well.
