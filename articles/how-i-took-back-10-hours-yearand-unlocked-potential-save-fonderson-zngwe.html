For immigrant doctors rebuilding their careers, one system at a time

When I restarted my medical career in the Netherlands, I had left for over 10 years and was expected to learn how the healthcare system worked all over again. What I didn’t expect was how much I would need to relearn how to work—efficiently, safely, and without burning out.

The clinical side wasn’t the problem. I knew how to diagnose, treat, listen, and care.

But the administration? That was another story.

Let me give you a few examples that, if you’re working in a second-language health system, might sound all too familiar:

1. The Referral Ping-Pong

Patients regularly ask for referrals to specialist X in hospital A. A few days later, I get a phone call:

“Actually, my neighbour had their knee surgery in hospital B—can you resend the referral there instead?”

This might seem small, but it happens almost every day.

2. The Letter B vs. S Problem

In the Dutch mental health system, there’s basic care (B) and specialist care (S). Sometimes I send a well-structured referral to the basic service, only to get a response a few days later:

“Can you resend this same letter but change the ‘B’ to ‘S’ so we can accept it?”

Same patient. Same information. Just a different letter. Again—sounds minor. But it’s time-consuming, repetitive, and in my honest opinion, completely avoidable.

3. The Endless Documentation Requests

Parents need a letter for school confirming their child has ADHD and requires headphones. Insurers request full documentation—but only for the left shoulder, only from the date of a specific car accident. Home care referrals. Disability assessments. Sick leave declarations. School absence letters. Fitness-to-work reports.

Eventhough there are regulations to limit the number of documentation requests, there is a certain expectations that patients and other instutions have that a general practitioner can just provide this.

The list goes on.

Now add a language barrier into the mix.

Writing these letters in Dutch, documenting in Dutch, explaining symptoms and summarizing complex care paths in Dutch, it’s not just mentally draining. It takes twice as long when you're working in your second language.

And I haven’t even touched on the emotional toll of always having to double check your spelling, sentence structure, or phrasing, just to ensure clarity and professionalism.

And while nothing will make that work disappear completely, I did find one tool that helped me take back control: #TextExpander. (www.textexpander.com)

In this newsletter, I’m showing you exactly how I saved 10 hours this year using this one tool. And why I believe it holds the potential to save up to 5 full days of my year as I continue to use it consistently.

What Is TextExpander?

It’s a simple typing shortcut tool. You create snippets (shortcuts) for the text you use all the time, and it expands into full sentences or paragraphs when you type a code like ;referralintro.

It's like having a second brain that types for you, one that’s reliable, consistent, and multilingual if you need it to be. I use textexpander in both English and in Dutch to save me time and make sure that I maintain a consistency in my approach with patients and colleagues alike.

What I Gained From It

Here’s what I actually saved from January to December 2024 (excluding July, when I was on holiday):

  • 10 hours saved (582+ minutes)

  • 580+ snippets expanded

  • 12-month usage streak

That might not sound huge, but it’s the beginning of something much bigger. With consistent use and better systems, I am on well on my way to use this tool and save myself up to 5 full working days per year. That’s real time—time to see patients better, think clearer, and just take back control of my own agenda. Imagine saving nearly 1 month of administration time a year. That is my goal.

Here’s Exactly How I Use It

1. Structured Referrals That Keep Me Focused

I write about 30 referrals a day. And I always begin with the same sentence:

"Dear colleague, I hereby refer you my xx-year-old patient with xx complaints. His/her medical history includes xxx. Upon examination, I found xxx. We require your further expertise for the following xxx."

I created a snippet called ;referralintro. It pops this out in seconds—and more importantly, keeps me focused.

True fact: I once almost sent a referral for the wrong patient. A colleague caught it. Since then, this intro acts as my mental anchor. It ensures I’m present with each case.

2. Acute Assessments Made Safer

In urgent cases, I use a snippet in Dutch when I make a rapid assessment of my patients in the emergency department to guide my clinical judgement: I can then edit accordingly to what I have assessed. Later once the patient is stabilised, I can go ahead and write a more elaborate assessment. This helps me to focus on the job at hand.

Even on hectic days, I know I haven’t missed the basics. This one is called ;urgentassessment.
3. Safety-Netting for Common Complaints
About 80% of my patients go home with advice and a safety net. So I built snippets in Dutch for the most common conditions:
Headache
Fever in a child
Throat pain
You don’t need to be fluent to sound fluent. These snippets help me communicate clearly, confidently, and consistently—with less stress and zero second-guessing.
4. Admin Made Bearable
When I took over the current practice I onw, I had to fill out the same information dozens of times: If I am honest, the administrative workload during the takeover was overwhelming. When I used textexpander, it was to provide me with quick and accurate information on topics like:
  • Secure email
  • Practice address
  • Chamber of commerce number (kvk)
  • Practice codes
  • Website links
Instead of searching or retyping, I just typed ;address, ;email, ;practicecode ;practicedetails or ;kvk and it was done.

Maintenance Tip: Review Monthly
Once a month, I scroll through my snippet list. I delete the ones I no longer use. I update anything outdated. And I often find ideas for new ones based on what I’ve been doing a lot that month.
This tiny habit keeps the tool clean, sharp, and always relevant.
Final Thoughts for immigrant Doctors
If you're building or rebuilding your medical career in a new country, efficiency isn’t optional, it’s essential. TextExpander has helped me:
  • Save time
  • Avoid errors
  • Communicate with clarity
  • And most importantly: reduce stress
This isn’t a magic fix. It’s a smart system. And it’s one small step toward taking back control of your time and your clinical practice.
