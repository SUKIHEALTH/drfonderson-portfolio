My gp-snippet: built to protect flow, not just save time

As a GP, I don’t struggle with what to do. I often struggle with how to communicate clinical inforamtion efficiently, clearly, and yes often doing so under pressure.

Most of the patients I see come in with non-urgent complaints: headaches, knee pain, shoulder issues, mild abdominal pain. The clinical assessment is usually straightforward. But the plan especially the communication, matters deeply.

I need to explain the next steps in a way that’s concise and correct. I need the patient to understand, even when our languages or expectations don’t fully align. And I need the documentation to reflect that.

Let me show you how it works

Before gp-snippet, I relied on memory

Like many immigrant doctors, I work in my second language. Medical Dutch is precise and slow to recall when you’re moving fast. I used to type common phrases by memory, every time.

Things like:

  • “Discussed alarm symptoms; patient advised to contact us if condition worsens”

  • “Lab results reviewed; patient understands next steps and will contact if needed”

  • “Lab tests ordered; patient to call one week after visit to discuss results”

I knew what needed to be said. But each time I stopped to type or translate, it pulled me out of flow.

When my snippet tool stopped working, I built my own

I’d been using a tool called TextExpander for years, until recently my electronic health record system or HIS for those living in the Netherlands stopped supporting it. Around the same time, I started exploring AI tools and saw a call for a hackathon sponsored by Bolt.New.

It inspired me to start building. And so I did. I built the first version of gp-snippet an app that lets you create, store, search, and paste trusted snippets directly into any (browser-based) EMR.

The best thing about it, I integrated AI to help ensure that I don't miss any relevant details.

Nowadays, most patients have access to their EHR which is great. So they too can go back to the consultation note and it is clear for them what we discussed, in particular the plan, what they can expect regarding their symptoms and how we arranged follow-up care.

The app requires no setup it allows a GP like me to create my own unique voice (AI-assisted of course) store it and when i use simple short keywords, the entire phrase pops up.

It’s not a shortcut. It’s clinical support

gp-snippet protects my flow, my documentation, and my headspace. It’s flexible, shareable, and evolving every week. And it’s especially useful for:

  • Doctors working in their second language

  • GPs who want consistency across common cases

  • Teams who want to onboard faster and safer

  • Anyone who’s lost time retyping what they already know

My hope? That this becomes standard practice

With rising documentation pressure, we need tools that help not hinder good care. My long-term goal is to see snippet habits taught in medical training.

Because clarity, consistency, and communication are clinical skills. And they deserve better support.
