Why I Built my Own AI Medical Transcription tool.

Created on 2025-09-24 22:26

Published on 2025-09-25 09:00

And why every foward-thinking medical practice should consider it.

As a doctor working in a second language a transcripton tool is a gamechanger for me. Let me share with you my journey.

The problem I face as a GP.

Last week, I spent 3 hours after my last patient documenting consultations. Three hours. I had 6 home visits next to over 10 consultations at the practice. That's time I could have spent with family, continuing education, or simply recharging for the next day of patient care.

Does this found familiar?

While solutions like #Juvoly have pioneered the medical transcription space in the Netherlands (and deserve credit for leading this transformation), I began wondering: 

If you don't already know, I'm all about being in complete control of my schedule, and having it dictated by administrative burden is one of the most frustrating realities I face in healthcare. I need to work flexibly, especially given how healthcare keeps pushing our limited resources to the breaking point. Understanding how AI tools actually function has been a complete game-changer for my work and life, so I went ahead and created another tool that actually works.
The "Why Not?" Moment
As a physician who's always been curious about technology, I asked myself a simple question: "If I can diagnose complex medical conditions, why can't I build a tool that understands my specific practice needs?"
So I did. Using OpenAI's APIs and some creative problem-solving, I built a custom medical transcription system that:
  • Transcribes Dutch/English consultations in real-time
  • Generates SOAP notes automatically
  • Creates safety net suggestions for every patient
  • Translates between Dutch and English on-demand
  • Costs a fraction of commercial solutions
  • Gives me complete control over my data and workflow
See the complete video below ; in less than 2 minutes the entire medical information is summarised as a SOAP entry and there is a nice added feature, it gives you safety net information that you can share with the patient. 
Look, this isn't about replacing me, it's about ensuring complete documentation in patient files. I too forget to mention things sometimes and having that information captured is essential for future visits and for when team members need to step in. Bottom line: I'm human, not perfect, and my tools don't pretend to be either.
The Revelation: It's Not About Replacing Anyone
This isn't about competing with established players or claiming superiority. Juvoly and similar platforms serve many practices excellently. This is about physician empowerment and practice autonomy.
Here's what I learned:
  • Control Matters: When you understand your tools, you can adapt them to your exact needs
  • Cost Efficiency: My solution costs ~€20/month vs. €200+ for commercial platforms
  • Customization: I can modify it for pediatrics, internal medicine, or any specialty
  • Data Sovereignty: My patient data stays exactly where it needs to be, safe and in no cloud space
  • Learning: Understanding AI makes me a better doctor in an AI-supported environment
The Reality Check
Let me be crystal clear: This isn't for every consultation or every doctor.
Some days, the AI transcription misses nuances. Complex multilingual consultations sometimes need manual review. Emergency situations require human focus, not technology.
But for 70-80% of my routine consultations? It's transformative.
The Bigger Picture: Physician-Led Innovation
We're at an inflection point in healthcare technology. We can either:
Option A: Wait for tech companies to solve our problems (and pay premium prices) 
Option B: Learn, adapt, and build solutions that truly serve our practice needs
I chose Option B. Not because I'm anti-commercial solutions, but because I believe doctors should understand and shape the AI tools we use daily.
What This Means for Your Practice
Whether you use tools like Juvoly, build your own solution, or stick with traditional methods, ask yourself:
  1. Do you understand how your AI tools make decisions? 
  2. Can you adapt them when your practice needs change? 
  3. Are you prepared for the next wave of AI healthcare tools?
  4. What would 2-3 hours of saved documentation time mean for your work-life balance?
The Future We're Building
I envision a healthcare system where:
  • Doctors are AI-literate collaborators, not passive users
  • Practices have choice and control over their technology stack
  • Innovation comes from the clinic floor, not just boardrooms
  • Technology amplifies our expertise rather than replacing our judgment
My Challenge to Fellow Physicians
You don't need to become a programmer. But consider:
  • Taking one AI course this year
  • Understanding how your current tools work
  • Asking harder questions of your technology vendors
  • Exploring what's possible when doctors lead innovation
The future of medical practice belongs to physicians who understand their tools deeply enough to shape them.
