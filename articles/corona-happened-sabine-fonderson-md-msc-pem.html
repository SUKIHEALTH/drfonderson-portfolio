Then Corona Happened

Created on 2020-03-24 14:48

Published on 2020-03-24 15:14

Today I got the news, negative for SARS-COVID. What a delight, but it does not explain the feeling of weakness, the strange taste in my mouth and the dry cough. 'No need to worry' is the response I get from colleagues and family 'at least it's not Corona'.

At 14:00 pm a Zoom meeting with colleagues. Although I have called in sick due to my general health, I can still 'function', I'm just not myself. More importantly I want to know how my fellow GP trainees are doing in the time of Corona.

We share our experiences, and like mine, there is not much to do in the GP practice we each work at. Most of us see up to 3 patients a day, a few phone calls to patients who are worried about whether they too may have Corona. The days are long.

Some have found ways to occupy themselves by creating a list of vulnerable elderly patients at home in order to get a hold of them to see how they are coping. Most GP practices have a 'Corona Consult' in the afternoon for about 2 hours where a GP dressed in protection gear from head to toe, remains in one room only to consult and examine patients with cough and fever.

It is all about Corona, in the media, from friends, families, neighbours and colleagues.

An interesting remark from one of my fellow GP trainee during the Zoom meeting, apparently there are patients presenting with pancreatitis-like symptoms who are also Covid-19 positive. Are these just spurious findings or is there a relationship? We won't know because those who have a suspected high risk of being infected with the virus are told to stay home for 14 days and unless their symptoms do not deteriorate, have no reason to visit their GP.

Then we talk about how Corona is affecting our work. Some of the GP trainees have volunteered to work in the emergency care department or intensive care units. They want to feel useful instead of hanging around in a practice and take phone calls. It makes sense they want to help, there is no denying that the numbers are rising, they will continue to rise for a while. In some hospitals, intensive care (IC) beds are fully occupied and make-shift IC departments are being created to meet the demands of patient care.

One GP trainee reminds us to take care of our own health, already in a hospital where he works, one third of the personnel are on sick leave because they are overworked. Not surprising to me. So while I am delighted I tested negative, it still does not explain my symptoms. I will do the right thing and call in sick, let my body recover, I will be needed soon best to recover now and be strong when Corona happens.
