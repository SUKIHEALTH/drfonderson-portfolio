Helping internationally trained doctors build their talent despite system delays

In March 2025, Medisch Contact published a report revealing that over 150 internationally trained doctors in the Netherlands are currently stuck in a prolonged waiting period, this wait can be up to 25 months before they can take the BI-toets, the final step before BIG registration.

The BI-toets is short for Beroepsinhoudelijke Toets, which basically means a Professional Medical Examination. It’s the final hurdle that internationally trained doctors must clear to prove they’re ready to work as doctors in the Dutch healthcare system. This test allows every doctor no matter their training background, can work in the Netherlands and meets the same standards.

The BI-toets has three parts:

  1. A general knowledge test

  2. A clinical reasoning test (how doctors think through medical cases)

  3. A practical exam is where candidates see simulated patients (trained actors) and demonstrate their consultation and clinical skills

The practical exam is the part that takes the most resources and it’s only offered at one university (Radboudumc in Nijmegen). Furthermore, only a limited number of candidates can take it a few times per year.

Until recently, internationally trained doctors had to take two exams: the AKV test (language and general skills) and the BI-toets. In 2024, the AKV was removed to help speed things up.

This deision worked very well so much so more doctors could apply for the BI-toets, but the number of available test slots hasn’t increased. As a result, some doctors are being told they’ll have to wait up to 25 months just to take the exam.

That’s over two years of waiting, with no real options for medical work in the meantime.

Once docotrs pass the BI-toet, they may need to do a short internship (coschappen), depending on their experience. After that, they can apply for the BIG-register — which officially makes them a licensed doctor in the Netherlands. Once a doctor is registered in the medical professional register can they start applying for jobs as physicians.

Why is this important for immingrant doctors at this moment?

During those 1 to 2 years of waiting, doctors often can’t work, can’t do research, and in some cases are told they’re overqualified for entry-level roles and underqualified for everything else.

This news sparked this article. I think it is a waste of talent. More needs to be done to help brilliant, motivated doctors stay active, stay seen, and stay ready, even when the system stalls.

As a former president of the Vereniging Buitenlands Gediplomeerde Artsen (VBGA), I have witnessed over 200 immigrant doctor s struggle through this system. I’ve sat with them during training sessions and listened as they described the pain of navigating the various exams and being left unsure of their career.

The consequences of sidelining hundreds of qualified medical professionals won’t be visible immediately. But make no mistake: we are heading toward a public health issue.

We’re standing at the intersection of three major threats:

  • An aging population with increasing care needs

  • Shrinking human capacity in the healthcare workforce

  • Continued underfunding across key healthcare facilities

In this reality, we simply cannot afford to keep skilled doctors waiting to use their talent.

Internationally trained doctors are not asking for shortcuts. They understand the need for high standards of care. But we must also remember that these are medically competent, multilingual, highly motivated dotors who are ready to work and be of use to society.

Yet the system delays them. And worse, makes them feel invisible.

This newsletter is for them, and for all immigrant doctors navigating medicine in a second language. If the system won’t make space for you yet, we’ll build one ourselves.

My Own Waiting Period

When I moved to the UK as a trained doctor, I had to pass the IELTS exams. Even though English is my first language, I did do my medical training in The Netherlands. It was frustrating. But during my two-month wait, I refused to let my knowledge gather dust.

Here’s what I did:

  • Wrote patient content for Hearst International

  • Edited protocols for health ministries in countries such as Cameroon and Dubai

  • Tutored international medical students online and helped them find internships in Africa

  • Built digital systems and templates to save time in clinical work and selling these on various platforms.

These experiences now form the foundation of the strategies I share with doctors who are faced with navigating a new career in a new country.

What You Can Do While Waiting for Medical profieciency tests?

Based on my experience with the VBGA and immigrant colleagues across Europe, here are four proven paths immigrant doctors can take, ethically, legally, and meaningfully, while waiting for their medical board registration.

1. Start a Micro-Clinic (Legally and Ethically)

Offer services like:

  • Health coaching

  • Lifestyle support

  • Chronic illness guidance

  • Patient education for other immigrants

With their own non-clinical practice, international doctors don’t need to diagnose or prescribe, but they can educate, support, coach and empower. This is the premise of my Launch your micro-clinic program, designed specifically for immigrant doctors building a career for themselves during the “waiting period.”

2. Use Learned Skills in Medical Writing and Editing

There’s growing demand for medically literate professionals who can:

  • Write patient-friendly articles

  • Translate complex letters or test results

  • Edit or create public health materials in various languages

This is what I did to earn income before I could practice. It also expanded my visibility and credibility.

3. Contribute to Research

Even without a board-certified registration, some academic departments will accept international doctors for:

  • Literature reviews

  • Scientific editing

  • Protocol support

Yes, it takes persistence. But I’ve helped doctors write outreach messages that work. I have reviewed CV's and helped to refine motivation letters in order to get into the door for an interview in a research department.

It does not stop there, once inside, internationally trained doctors will need to prove their abilities to write content that is made for publication.

An Extra Language Is An Asset

Explain Medical Results to Patients in Their Own Language

Even before the're licensed to treat, internationally trained doctors can act as a trusted interpreter of medical meaning.

Many patients, especially in expat or underserved communities struggle to understand medical information such as imaging results, blood tests and diagnoses or specialists letters.

Also with the ageing population and family members having to take time off to take care of frail parents, a doctor who is waiting to be registered can act as a consultant or patient liason in various clinical settings.

This builds trust, reputation, and value, long before any formal practice begins.

You’re Not Just a Candidate — You’re Still a Doctor

Too many brilliant doctors feel forced into invisibility. But their knowledge hasn’t vanished. Their training hasn’t expired. And their value hasn’t diminished.

Let’s use this time. Let’s build them up so that they can practice medicine. Because if we do not fill the gap in medicine now, the next generation of health professionals will have it even harder than it is already.
