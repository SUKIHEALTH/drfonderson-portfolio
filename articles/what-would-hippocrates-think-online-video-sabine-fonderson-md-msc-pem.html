What would Hippocrates think of online video consultations?

Dr. Sabine Fonderson

Physician · Health AI Innovator · Keynote Speaker

Created on 2016-12-10 00:21

Published on 2019-12-10 23:31

Hippocrates believed that everything that happens to our body is connected to nature, to earth. He described that the four bodily fluids blood, yellow bile, black bile and phlegm were related to air, fire, earth and water respectively.

Galen elaborated further stating that blood was related to spring, yellow bile to summer, black bile to autumn and phlegm to winter. Ideler, took it a step further and concluded that the four bodily fluids were related to one’s personality. Those that produced too much blood were happy, energetic, compared to those that produced yellow bile who were prone to bitterness and depression was often seen in those who produced more black bile, consequently, those with too much phlegm were low in spirit and apathetic.

In time people would stop to believe that spirits, seasons and nature played a crucial part in the maintenance of health. The new school of thought paved a way for scientists like Descartes a mechanist himself, to discover that the body was in fact made up of organs, each working individually to keep everything in balance. Liken this thinking to that of a mechanical clock with various cogs that work in synergy to make the hands move and tell time.

Throughout centuries, medical science has been established from observations, making assumptions and building on a system of knowledge. However, what if the way in which these observations are made change. For instance, what if implementing new tools could lead to different interpretations of the observations, does that mean the old way was not valid? It was not until the 19th century when new diagnostic tools such as the stethoscope and microscope were invented that diagnosis became more specific and very interesting. The specificity revolution was born at the time in which diseases were seen as transient processes related to time and place. It is at this time that medical observations required a certain skill and being a doctor was a noble thing, one that required craft only made accessible to a handful of real scientist. But even then, John Snow faced critique from his contemporaries who were cautious to believe that the outbreak of cholera was caused by water instead of air particles.

It is the perseverance and methodological manner in which other scientists and health professionals like Newton and Einstein who would cemented the way modern science is practiced today. Doctors have been for years been taught to gather knowledge from objective observations, perform tests validated under stringent conditions and provide diagnosis with near perfect precision.

Where does telemedicine come in? Can objective observations be made through a screen? Contemporary doctors would argue that this is not possible, medicine cannot be practiced without physically seeing the patient.

Without stating the obvious, there are many medical interventions that cannot and should not be performed online. That being said, we should embrace online video consultations. They not only facilitate the interjection of patients and their doctors, but on the short, medium and even long run it can save lives, reduce stress and increase health performance

Let's go back to the time of Hippocrates where health was seen as a part of a entity, intertwined with seasons, spirits, natures, a complex entity. Online medical consultations can take this complexity into account. What once was causing time delays, frustrations can be resolved within minutes because it is convenient for both patient and health professionals. That is what is ultimately driving our drive forward to optimal health and providing great care. That it can happen at the time when the patient needs it the most. And when that time, comes, we best be prepared to give that time and no matter how complex it it, we have the tools to accessible at our finger tips to make them better.

I want to hear from you. What would you do if a patient preferred to chat or consult with you online for some one or more medical issues?
