6 steps doctors should consider when making a career switch

Dr. Sabine Fonderson

Physician · Health AI Innovator · Keynote Speaker

Created on 2016-04-12 09:54

Published on 2016-04-12 09:56

There are a growing number of medical doctors looking for different career paths. In the UK, a Quarterly tracker survey published by the BMA states that in November 2015  “over 66% of respondents cited work conditions and changes to the NHS as the top two factors that influenced considerations about their career”.

Many doctors are becoming vocal about their work conditions and sharing their experiences on social media. One such example is that of the disillusioned doctor who poignantly recounts her experience she had during a job interview and how she coped with the rejection. You can read about her stories after she decided to quit medicine altogether in her blog

It is not easy for doctors to switch jobs let alone find something completely unrelated to what they have been doing for years and then get rejected. In fact it is not a pleasant experience for anyone. So what should a doctor do when making that crucial career switch? There are obviously no single answer or blueprint to follow because it is based on the individual doctor, the motivation behind the switch and what they are willing to do in order to further their careers. What I will do is break it down into 6 steps starting from that moment you have decided to switch careers regardless of your motivation.

  1. Don't feel guilty. Instead of looking for approval from colleagues, friends or family start looking for results. Guilt will lead to doubt which will lead to more guilt and it becomes a vicious cycle. One that you may have been in for a while until you made the tough decision to switch careers.
  2. Start planning. Get a pen and paper, go online and do a search at different job sites. Look for various job offers, things that you would never have even thought of doing and tick the ones that resonate with you. Go through the job descriptions thoroughly. For example some job titles may not initially spark an interest but critically going through the job description and requirements may give you ideas and prompt other searches. Plus, be honest about how much you want to earn. This can become a relevant concern when you switch careers. In most cases you will start at an entry level and have to face a serious drop in your salary. Luckily many entry level positions offer fantastic prospects to move up the ladder.
  3. Update your CV. Once you are looking seriously at other job opportunities then you know your CV needs some attention too. It can happen that you stumble across a job description which you think is perfect only to realise the opening closes in a few hours. Your outdated CV is in serious need of a makeover. By the time you get it updated you may have missed the deadline. So at the earliest stage, update your CV. Ask a family member or friend to go through the document and give you feedback. You will be happy they picked on up small glitches in your CV. A cover letter comes in really handy once you have selected a job post to apply to. This brings me to the next point
  4. Do your homework on the company. It is important that the moment you send your CV for the role you also attach a cover letter that is tailored for that company. Avoid generic cover letters. If you can, give them a phone call and talk to a representative. Let them know you saw the job opening and that you will be sending your CV across. This could be a good opportunity to process your application quicker or gain more information about the role itself. Plus, if you realise at the end of the conversation that it actually is not a match then you can quickly move on to other job applications.
  5. Prepare for the interview It is really important you do not skip this part. There are numerous resources on interview techniques which you can find on various websites or books. I will not get into all these tips but there is one important question that will be asked: ‘Why you want to make a career switch’. It will make sense to think really carefully about the response you give. You may consider saying things like you've ‘worked 8 Christmases in a row and you want to finally spend the holidays with loved ones’ or that ‘The nanny gets more quality time with your kid and that is unacceptable’. These are all understandable reasons to look for another job and can be mentioned in passing. Instead my advice is to focus on skills you have that will add value to the company. Make a list of skills that you have developed such as report writing, IT knowledge, audits, courses you have taken or even taught and any public speaking engagements. Give as much examples as possible to illustrate what you can offer to the team. Having examples of your transferable skills adds depth to the interview. That is why it's important to prepare this in advance.
  6. Be prepared for rejections. Do not let rejections undermine your efforts. Instead make a phone call or send an email requesting why you did not get the job. The chances are small that you will get a detailed answer of why you were not offered the role, but it does pay off to know what it is you may need to work on or develop in order to make future job applications successful.

Quick tips for doctors who want to switch careers

  1. Go through different job sites and look for roles that you would not even consider then look closely at the job descriptions and requirement. Select a list of key words and criterias that you can use to update your CV and cover letters.
  2. Do your homework on the company you are applying to work with, the team involved and what you will be expected to do. It can happen that you the job is right for both you and the recruiter but you will have to relocate.
  3. Start looking at ways to diversify your skills and talents. A way to do this without making the complete switch is to do some freelance work. Doctors have many skills that they can transfer in freelance posts and I will cover this topic in a future post.

Good luck in your career switch and if you have any tips to share with other doctors in similar situations please share them.
