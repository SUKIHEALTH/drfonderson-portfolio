How I used an AI tool to prescribe safely in a new healthcare system

Created on 2026-02-11 22:23

Published on 2026-02-12 15:00

When you practice medicine in a country that isn’t your own, prescribing feels different.

You double-check everything. Not because you don’t know the medicine, but because you’re navigating unfamiliar legal formats, pharmacy expectations, and insurance documentation standards at the same time.

Working with international patients and travel insurance partners at Expat Health Clinic , I kept noticing how much effort it takes to write prescriptions and make sure it ends up at the right pharmacy at the right time. Especially when my regular patient record system did not communicate with the hundreds of pharmacies around the country.

Formatting requirements. Interaction checks across incomplete medication lists. Ensuring the document is legally accepted by local pharmacies. Making sure insurers receive clean documentation.

Individually, these are small steps. Together, they create friction and risk.

So I built a digital prescription generator for our clinic.

It allows multiple medications on one form, generates pharmacy-ready PDFs, and works on mobile during home visits. More importantly, it includes built-in safety checks: drug interaction alerts and dosage support integrated directly into the workflow.

Yes, it uses AI.

But I don’t see AI as the headline. I see it as reinforcement.

As immigrant doctors, we are constantly translating, language, systems, expectations. That translation takes energy. When used carefully, AI can reduce that mental load and act as a structured second check. Not replacing clinical judgment, but supporting it.

Recently, I treated a tourist from latin America with a complex medical history who needed urgent medication. Within minutes, the prescription was formatted correctly for Dutch pharmacies, interaction-checked, and ready to share with the insurance partner. The pharmacy accepted it immediately. I did not have any confusion, no continuous editing or pressing on the tab button to align the Rx to the dosage. Most importantly there was no delay.

And there was a time a patient from America stopped her anti-depressants abruptly because she ran out of her stock and was not able to find a local GP to prescribe a new refill.

After a teleconsultation and verifying her previous medication prescription, I used this AI-enhanced app to get her the prescription she needed with the right information to a pharmacy and she could get back on track with her mental health.

That experience reinforced something for me.

Digital tools in healthcare should not exist to look innovative. They should exist to quietly reduce risk at high-friction moments.

This prescription generator is one small part of a broader mission I care deeply about: building practical, clinician-designed systems that help doctors, especially those working across borders practice with more safety and confidence.
