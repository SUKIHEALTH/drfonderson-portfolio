Tired? Good. Now let AI handle the noise

Dr. Sabine Fonderson

Physician · Health AI Innovator · Keynote Speaker

The real doctor
Helping doctors rebuild their careers after immigration.

Published on ---

The most dangerous moment in medicine isn't not knowing the answer, it's being too tired to ask the right question, and AI can quietly hold the line for you.

Did I miss anything today?

If you're a doctor, you've asked yourself that question. Probably more than once. Probably tonight.

I think about it on the drive home. Did I catch the red flag? Did I explain it clearly enough? Was that "probably nothing" actually nothing?

Here's the truth nobody says out loud: the thing every doctor fears most isn't a hard case. It's making a mistake we could have caught. Not knowing an answer. Being caught off guard when a treatment turns. None of us walks into the clinic thinking "today I'll mess up and end up at a tribunal." We walk in wanting the opposite, and then we do it 30 times before lunch.

So let me ask the uncomfortable question.

Is it a weakness to admit we get tired? Are we less human because we use tools that help us?

I don't think so. I think the opposite is true.

Fatigue is not a character flaw. It's information. And when we ignore it, it doesn't stay fatigue, it slides into burnout, and getting back from burnout takes weeks, sometimes months. That's not sustainable for us, and it's certainly not safe for our patients.

This is even heavier when you practice in a second language, like I do. The cognitive load is doubled. You're not just reasoning through the medicine, you're reasoning through the medicine and checking your wording, your phrasing, your safety-netting, in a language that isn't your first. By the end of the day there's not much left in the tank.

So I started asking a different question. Not "how do I work harder," but "how do I take the noise off my plate so the tired version of me still makes the safe call?"

We live in a world drowning in information. Guidelines, updates, differentials, acute cases. A good AI tool doesn't diagnose for you. It doesn't treat for you. What it does is filter the noise, so that at the moment that matters, you know you did everything reasonable to rule out the worst-case scenario.

The one thing you can use today

Build (or use) a simple AI triage helper that acts as a second pair of eyes, not a decision-maker.

You don't need to be a programmer. You can start with an existing tool, or stand up a basic one yourself. The point is to have something that, when you feed it the case, gives you back:

  • Red flags you might want to double-check

  • A differential you can sanity-check your own thinking against

  • A clear, plain-language explanation for the patient, with safety-netting built in

Notice what that list is not. It's not "tell me the diagnosis." It's "show me what I might be too tired to see right now." That distinction is everything.

The reality check

Let me be crystal clear: this isn't for everyone, and it isn't foolproof.

If you prompt it badly, the tool will happily hand you a confident diagnosis, and that's exactly the trap. The moment you treat it as the answer instead of the noise filter, you've made it more dangerous than helpful. You have to see beyond the output. The clinical judgement stays with you. Always. The tool is a safety net, not a parachute.

So the skill isn't "use AI." The skill is "use AI without outsourcing your thinking to it."

This is the whole thesis I keep coming back to: AI should give us back the time and headspace to practice the art of medicine, the listening, the human read of the room, the judgement. The tools handle the noise. We stay human. That's the trade I'm interested in.

If you want to see what this looks like

I built a working version of exactly this, a GP triage helper that follows NHG standards, flags red flags, drafts a patient explanation with safety-netting, and suggests first-line management. It runs on Hugging Face. It does not diagnose; it removes the noise so you can do the deciding.

If you'd like a walkthrough of how it works, or how to build your own, drop me a DM or tag a colleague who's running on empty this week.

So here's my question for you: what's the one task that drains you most by the end of the day, and what would change if you could quietly hand the noise of it to a tool, and keep the judgement for yourself?

I'm human, not perfect, and I'd genuinely like to know what's eating your evenings.
