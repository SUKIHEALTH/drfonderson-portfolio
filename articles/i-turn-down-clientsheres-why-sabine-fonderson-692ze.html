I turn down clients...here's why.

Dr. Sabine Fonderson

Physician · Health AI Innovator · Keynote Speaker

Created on 2023-11-12 22:23

Published on 2023-11-14 11:30

It was my first week as a locum doctor, I had barely settled down in my new flat and my phone was on fire.

Multiple recruiters were telling me how needed I was. I could work anywhere I wanted and of course make a lot of money.

Before I knew it every job opportunity felt tempting. And before long I had said "Yes" to every availability that I could fill.

Back then I had no responsibilities except for taking care of myself. But then again, I don't understand why I felt the urge to say yes to most of the posts.

There are several factors I think that played a role

  1. A sense of urgency. The recruiters made me feel that the posts would be swept away by another qualified doctor. I can't exactly recall how they did it, but I needed to let them know within a certain timeframe otherwise it would be gone. And it did happen that when I later called back the post had been taken.

  2. I was eager to get as much experience as possible. The more I worked the better it would be on my CV and as a recruiter told me, within 2 weeks of locum work, I could even ask for a higher hourly rate. It worked, before 1 month was up, I doubled my hourly rate and was upgraded to a senior doctor level. I am not sure if that reflected my skills at that time but I did use that money to take on as many courses as possible.

Then I took on different roles, care of the elderly one week, then then next it was acute medical unit, then a week later I was in the internal medical wards and two weeks later doing a few nightshifts in the pediatrics ward.

It was a mess. I had no structure and whilst the pay was good, I felt that I was not doing the best I could. Not to mention the endless emails and messages I received from recruiters.

Yes it as my fault that I signed up in the first place, but I had no idea how overwhelming it would get.

Finally I decided to take a break and sort out what it was that I really wanted to do.

The choice was clear to me because when I looked at the courses that I was taking to continue developing professionally they had one thing in common - emergency care.

I decided to ditch the recruiters and the environment I lived in.

Saying no to locum posts was the most liberating thing that I did because it gave me time to focus on what I wanted to do.

Since then I have not hesitated turning down clients that I know will not get the best out of me. Even if we start a working relationship - it can happen that things take an unexpected turn. I do not hesitate to turn them down.

And I am sure the opposite is true.

I want to stress that this applies for business clients and not for my patients. Although I have refused certain things. For example, I was once asked by a client whom I had given medical advice incidentally. This client reached out to me to write a medical report stating why they needed to be housed in a flat with two toilets due to their gastrointestinal complaints. The client was willing to pay for this report.

Whilst I feel sad for the client, it was not something I felt was in my scope of work and I turned that client down.

I have more examples of clients I turned down.
