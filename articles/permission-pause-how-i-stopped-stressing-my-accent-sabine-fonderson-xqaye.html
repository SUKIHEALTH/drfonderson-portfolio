Permission to Pause: How I Stopped Stressing About My Accent in Consultations

When I call a patient from the waiting room, I already know what’s coming. The walk from my office to the waiting area is about 15 steps and during those 15 steps, my brain processes about 100 pieces of information. The patient's name, their age, their problem list, their medication, what time it is, whether I’m already running behind...

And then, my French brain kicks in. English is my first language, French my second and then Dutch. Somewhere between my office and the waiting room, I manage to add my French accent to their Dutch name. I call it out, and nine times out of ten, the patient has to correct me as they stand up.

It used to frustrate me. I'd start the consultation already feeling a bit defeated.

Now? I simply smile and say, as we walk back to my office:

"Your name sounds different when I say it with my French accent, right?"

They laugh, I laugh and suddenly, we’re both human. The consultation starts on a lighter, more forgiving note.

It's okay to pause, laugh, or reframe

Working as a doctor in your second or third language means you will miss things sometimes. For example, patients speaking in dialects I barely understand.

Before, I used to nod along, too scared to interrupt. Now, I look them straight in the eye and say: "I have no idea what you just said. Can you say it another way?"

It stuns them for a second but then they reframe, slow down, and appreciate that I'm trying to understand them, not just rushing through the checklist. That's trust. And trust buys you the time and grace you need when language barriers sneak in.

Permission to Rewind (Even Mid-Sentence)

Sometimes, when a patient starts telling me their complaints, my brain zooms ahead, trying to solve the puzzle before they've even finished. I piece things together too quickly, get excited, and end up giving a big explanation...

...only to realize the patient is staring at me, slightly confused.

Now I simply stop and say:

"Sorry, I was ranting — is this actually helpful for you? Or were you thinking about something else?"

Patients appreciate the honesty. You're not lecturing, you are on the path of shared-decision making which is a collaboration.

Starter Sentences for Doctors working in a second language

Here are some of my favorite "permission to pause" sentences you can use when the consultation gets to a point you know you need to take a second:

  • "Let me rephrase that, just to be sure we're on the same page."

  • "Sorry, I just had a brain-switch moment — can we go back one step?"

  • "I'm not sure I understood you correctly. Can you explain it one more time, in your own words?"

  • "Sometimes my second language brain gets creative — let me double-check with you."

  • "I might say this a little differently than you're used to — tell me if it sounds unclear."

Final Thought

Working in medicine in a second (or third) language isn't about speaking perfectly. It's about listening carefully, laughing a little, and giving yourself permission to pause when you need it.

Patients don’t want perfect doctors. They want real ones.

And being real with an accent, pauses, rewinds and all is more powerful than you think.
